One system of record, from assets to assurance

Roles & access

Department member — sees records owned by their department only. Read and update access follows the record owner.

Head of Department (HOD) — manages their department's records, users, saved views and the change log. Cross-department data stays hidden.

Superadmin — full platform access, including departments, role mapping and Django admin. Superadmin actions are still written to the change log.

Notifications & escalation

The bell shows unread notifications for your department. Email alerts can be enabled per user in Settings.

HODs and superadmins can also write and send their own announcements — see Sending notifications below.

Overdue controls, expiring policies, breached KRIs and incident SLAs escalate automatically to the record owner, then the department HOD, then the platform escalation list.

Incident severity sets the response SLA: critical 1h, high 4h, medium 24h, low 72h. Personal-data breaches also start a 72-hour notification clock.

Sending notifications

Most notifications in GRC Nexus are raised automatically when a record needs attention. An announcement is the opposite: a message a person writes on purpose — a phishing drill, a policy reminder, a maintenance window, a change of process. It is delivered in the app like any other notification, and optionally by email, so you do not have to rely on a separate mailing list.

How to send one

  1. Open Notifications, then Send notification (also in the user menu).
  2. Write a title and an optional message. The title is what people see in the bell.
  3. Under Who should receive it?, pick one audience and tick the departments or people it applies to.
  4. Leave Also send by email on if people should get a copy in their inbox.
  5. Press Send notification. The summary panel on the right shows the audience before you send.

Email delivery setup

GRC Nexus can hand messages to an SMTP server so notifications and announcements also reach people's inboxes. The settings live in the database, so they can be changed from the UI without editing .env or restarting the container. Only a superadmin can change them.

Configure it

  1. Go to Settings → Email delivery → Configure.
  2. Turn on Deliver email through this SMTP server.
  3. Enter the host, port, encryption and — for a relay that needs a login — the username and password.
  4. Set the from name and from address recipients should see.
  5. Save, then use Send test message to prove delivery before relying on it.
  6. Confirm the result: the page reports how many messages the relay accepted.

When it is off
Nothing breaks: messages are written to the container log instead, and in-app notifications are unaffected.
Per-user choice
Each user can switch email off in Settings. Automatic alerts also respect each person's notification preferences.

If email does not arrive

  • Nothing was sent at all — check the mode banner at the top of the Email delivery page. If SMTP is disabled, only the log is written.
  • The test fails — host, port or encryption is wrong, or the relay refused the connection. Port 25 is often blocked outbound; use 587 with STARTTLS for a hosted provider.
  • The test succeeds but a person gets nothing — check the address on their account and whether they turned email off in Settings.
  • Mail is accepted but never lands — look at the mail server's own log and the recipient mailbox. On the bundled Ubuntu relay, the deployment notes in docs/EMAIL.md cover Postfix, Dovecot and webmail.
